# Anti-AI Voice Guide — Echoes (EN)

*Detecting and eliminating AI-generated writing patterns. Calibrated for Eros, first-person male, literary register.*
*Derived from: Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing" + [Humanizer skill v2.3](https://github.com/blader/humanizer) + Nic's actual written chapters.*
*For: writer agent (prevention), reviewer agent (audit), author (self-check).*
*This is the PRIMARY version — chapters are written in English first, then adapted to Italian.*

---

## Why this document

A chapter can hit every commercial pattern — NR 85%+, hook ≤10 words, active motor — and still fail if it **reads like AI**. Eros readers pay for Nic's voice. If that voice sounds generated, the contract breaks.

This document identifies AI writing signals in literary English fiction, first-person male POV, and provides rules to eliminate them.

**This is not a style guide.** Nic's voice is defined in the arc structures and the bible. This document protects that voice from the mechanical patterns a writer agent tends to produce.

**Companion document:** `anti-ai-voice-guide.md` (Italian version) covers Italian-specific signals for the translation/adaptation phase.

## PART 1 — AI signals in English prose

These come directly from the [Humanizer skill](https://github.com/blader/humanizer) and Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing", filtered and recalibrated for literary fiction rather than expository text.

### 1.1 Significance inflation

AI puffs up the importance of everything. In expository text this produces "pivotal moment" and "testament to." In fiction it produces narrators who treat every scene as historic.

**Flag words:**
- "a pivotal / crucial / fundamental moment"
- "represents / embodies / symbolizes"
- "underscores / highlights the importance of"
- "a turning point in"
- "deeply rooted"
- "marks a before and after"
- "something shifted / something changed in that moment"

**How Nic actually writes:** He minimizes. When something is enormous, he tells it small. "Ce told me she's a lesbian. In the office. On a Friday night. Wearing a hot pink top." Not: "Ce revealed something fundamental that would forever change the dynamics of our group."

**Rule:** If a sentence contains "fundamental", "crucial", "pivotal", "represents" — rewrite it with a concrete fact.

### 1.2 Participial depth-faking

AI tacks present participle phrases onto sentences to simulate depth. The Humanizer skill flags "-ing" endings in expository text. In fiction, the same pattern appears as emotional appendices.

**AI pattern:**
- "She sat down, looking at him with a mix of desire and fear."
- "He closed the door, leaving behind everything he'd been."
- "He took her hand, feeling the warmth of her skin."

**How Nic writes:** "She sat down. Looked at him. He didn't know if it was desire or fear — probably both, probably neither." The thought forks. It doesn't summarize.

**Rule:** Max 1 participial emotional appendix per 2,000 words. If the participle "explains" the emotion of the previous clause, cut it. The emotion must emerge from the action, not from a grammatical attachment.

### 1.3 AI vocabulary words

The Humanizer skill lists high-frequency AI words. These are even more damaging in fiction because they break the illusion of a human narrator.

**Kill on sight:**
- additionally, furthermore, moreover
- delve, navigate (figurative), landscape (abstract)
- tapestry, interplay, intricacies
- foster, cultivate, garner
- underscore, showcase, highlight (as verbs)
- pivotal, crucial, vital, enduring
- testament, beacon
- it's important to note that
- in this context
- ultimately

**How Nic thinks:** He doesn't use connector words. He juxtaposes. "The office was empty. Just the hum of the AC. The clock ticking. My heart beating too fast for a Monday morning." Three sentences, zero connectors, the reader builds the link.

**Rule:** If any word from this list appears in Nic's internal monologue, it's wrong. These words belong in essays, not in someone's head.

### 1.4 Copula avoidance

AI substitutes elaborate constructions for simple "is/are/has." The Humanizer skill flags "serves as", "stands as", "functions as."

**AI pattern:**
- "She served as his anchor."
- "The apartment functioned as a refuge."
- "The silence stood as proof."

**How Nic writes:** "Ce is the most brilliant person I've ever hired." Not "Ce represents the most brilliant resource on the team." "Is" is the strongest verb in English. Use it.

**Rule:** If "is" works, use "is."

### 1.5 Rule of Three

AI forces ideas into groups of three. In fiction this produces triads of emotions, descriptions, and observations that feel assembled rather than felt.

**AI pattern:**
- "Desire, fear, and vulnerability."
- "She was beautiful, intelligent, and dangerous."
- "He felt the warmth, the scent, and the weight of her body."

**How Nic writes:** He lists, yes — but asymmetrically. "Ninety-six hours. One hundred and seventeen minutes. Not that I'm counting." Three elements, but the third breaks the pattern with self-deprecation. Or two: "Not tears of anger or frustration. Tears of pure pain." Two, not three. A third would be too many.

**Rule:** If there are 3 items in a list, the third must break the pattern (irony, register shift, unexpected detail) — or cut to two. Two is almost always better than three.

### 1.6 Em dash overuse

AI uses em dashes as universal punctuation. The Humanizer skill flags this for expository text. In fiction it's worse because it flattens the narrator's rhythm.

**AI pattern:** 3+ em dashes per paragraph. Used for asides, emphasis, dramatic pauses — all at once — without distinction.

**How Nic writes:** He uses em dashes for thought-forks: "Didn't know if it was desire or fear — probably both." But for pauses he uses ellipsis. For asides he uses parentheses (the secondary thought that opens and closes). Three different tools, not one.

**Rule:** Max 3 em dashes per 2,000-word chapter. For pauses: ellipsis. For asides: parentheses. For forks: em dash.

### 1.7 Negative parallelisms

"Not only... but also...", "It's not just about X, it's about Y." AI overuses these constructions.

**AI pattern:**
- "It wasn't just desire — it was need."
- "Not only had she changed his life, but she'd changed his understanding of himself."

**How Nic writes:** He states things once. "It was need." Done. The reader doesn't need the contrast scaffolding.

**Rule:** Zero "not only... but also" constructions. Zero "it's not just X, it's Y." State the thing.

### 1.8 Elegant variation (synonym cycling)

AI has repetition-penalty code that causes excessive synonym substitution. In fiction, this means the same character gets called by different labels within paragraphs.

**AI pattern:**
- "Nic looked at her. The engineer felt something shift. The consultant reached for her hand. The father of GioGio understood."

**How Nic writes:** "I looked at her. Something shifted. I reached for her hand." First person solves most of this, but it still appears in how Nic refers to others. Ce is Ce. Not "the young American", "the new hire", "the green-eyed girl" in rotation.

**Rule:** Each character has ONE name per scene. No rotating labels. If you need variety, use pronouns.

## PART 2 — AI signals in first-person fiction

These patterns don't exist in the Humanizer skill because it targets expository text. In first-person fiction — especially erotic/emotional — AI has different and more insidious tells.

### 2.1 Over-articulated emotions

The single worst AI signal in fiction. AI names and explains every emotion the moment the character feels it.

**AI pattern:**
- "I felt a mix of desire and shame, as if my body wanted one thing and my mind another."
- "Jealousy cut through me like a blade, immediately followed by guilt for feeling it."
- "I felt an emotion I couldn't define — something between gratitude and fear."

**How Nic writes:** "Jealousy? No. Or at least, not the kind I expected. Not the possessive, territorial kind. Something subtler — the feeling of being excluded from something important." The emotion doesn't arrive labeled. It arrives as a question, gets denied, gets corrected, gets specified through a concrete image. The process is visible.

**Rule:** Never name an emotion and explain it in the same sentence. Either name it and leave it, or show it without naming it. The reader isn't stupid.

### 2.2 Too-orderly introspection

AI produces thoughts that proceed in logical order: premise → development → conclusion. Human thoughts don't work that way.

**AI pattern:**
- Paragraph 1: "I realized I loved her."
- Paragraph 2: "This meant I had to face my fears."
- Paragraph 3: "I decided I would talk to her."

**How Nic thinks:** "I open Excel out of habit — my refuge, the place where chaos becomes a grid. But today the columns stay empty. There's no formula for what's happening." The thought starts from a concrete action (opening Excel), detours into a personal metaphor (the refuge), and arrives at an emotional admission (no formula). It's not linear. It's associative.

**Rule:** First-person thought must contain at least 1 detour, 1 contradiction, or 1 irrelevant detail per 500 words. Perfectly ordered thought is the strongest AI signal in fiction.

### 2.3 Symmetrical dialogue

AI produces exchanges where every line responds perfectly to the previous one, with matching length and register.

**AI pattern:**
```
"I love you," she said.
"I love you too," he replied.
"I'm scared," she whispered.
"Don't be scared," he reassured her.
```

**How Eros dialogue works:**
```
"So," I say. "The ninety-six hours are up."
"Yes," Ce says.
"And?"
```

Total asymmetry. Nic talks long, Ce answers short. Nic asks a one-word question. Nobody "reassures" anyone. People don't respond in kind — they respond sideways, late, to the wrong question.

**Rule:** In every exchange of 4+ lines, at least 1 line must be asymmetric: shorter than expected, off-topic, or a non-answer.

### 2.4 Generic romance body language

AI describes bodies with a limited, predictable vocabulary, especially in erotic scenes.

**AI body vocabulary:**
- "soft curves", "silky/velvet skin", "eyes that sparkled"
- "a moan escaped her lips"
- "their bodies moved in unison"
- "a wave of pleasure"
- "his touch was electric"
- "he lost himself in her"

**How Nic writes:** The body is specific, sometimes technical, sometimes comic. "We come too fast. Too much hunger." No poetry — honesty. Sex in Eros is told as fact, with its awkwardness, its failures, its ridiculous moments. "We need more water and less sand in our asses."

**Rule:** Every intimate scene must contain at least 1 awkward moment, 1 practical/technical detail, or 1 joke. If the sex is only beautiful, it's AI.

### 2.5 Missing calibrated profanity

AI either avoids swearing entirely or distributes it mechanically (one every N paragraphs, like a timer). Nic swears — "fuck", "shit", "assholes" — but with surgical precision.

**AI pattern:**
- Zero profanity (too clean)
- Evenly distributed profanity (too mechanical)
- Profanity only at "intense" moments (too predictable)

**How Nic talks:** "You didn't need to know she was a lesbian to not hurt her. You just needed to not be assholes." The swear word lands at the end of a lucid argument. It's not venting — it's a conclusion. Nic is vulgar when he's precise, not when he's angry.

**Rule:** 3-6 instances of profanity per 2,000-word chapter. Irregular distribution. At least 1 must land in a calm moment, not at an emotional peak.

### 2.6 Reflective post-scene closings

AI closes scenes with a paragraph that "processes" what just happened. This is the most common and most damaging signal for pacing.

**AI pattern:**
- "As I watched her sleep, I understood that nothing would ever be the same."
- "In that moment I knew my life had changed forever."
- "I stood there, in the silence, processing everything that had happened."

**How Nic closes:** With an image, a fact, or a joke. Never with reflection. "The two women in his life, dancing. He doesn't need anything else." Or: "Achievement unlocked: Quadrato." The scene ends ON the scene, not after it.

**Rule:** The last paragraph of every chapter must not contain verbs like "understood", "realized", "knew", "grasped", "it dawned on me." If the character "understands" something, the reader must have understood it 3 lines earlier.

### 2.7 Smooth transitions

AI connects every scene to the next with fluid transitions. Real life doesn't have transitions.

**AI pattern:**
- "The hours that followed passed in a blur."
- "When we finally arrived..."
- "The next day, as I..."

**How Nic writes:** Hard cut. Blank line. "---". Then: "8:15 AM. The office starts filling up." No transition. The reader jumps in time with the character. The gap is part of the narration.

**Rule:** Use hard cuts (---) instead of narrative transitions. If it takes more than 10 words to get from one scene to the next, cut and restart.

### 2.8 Everyone sounds the same

AI struggles to differentiate character voices. In a chapter with 3-4 speaking characters, everyone uses the same register.

**How Eros differentiates (WORK example):**
- **Nic:** Lists, parentheticals, IT/gaming metaphors. "Not that I'm counting."
- **Ce:** American slang, short sentences, EN/IT code-switching. "The sensory kink."
- **Vi:** Corporate register, surgical sentences, control. "We spent the night together."
- **Angi:** Emotional, laughs and cries, long sentences that pile up. "I want everyone and I want us to be okay and I want nobody to hurt anyone ever again."

**Rule:** In every dialogue scene, the reader must be able to identify who's speaking without dialogue tags. If they can't, the voices are too similar. Test: cover the names and reread.

## PART 3 — AI signals specific to Eros

Patterns that emerge when an LLM tries to write as Nic.

### 3.1 Forced gaming metaphors

AI knows Nic uses gaming metaphors and stuffs them everywhere, even where they don't belong.

**AI pattern:** A gaming metaphor every 300 words. "Achievement unlocked" in every chapter. "Boss fight" for every conflict.

**How Nic uses them:** Rarely. 2-3 per chapter, max. They arrive in moments of self-deprecation, when Nic distances himself from what he feels. They're a defense mechanism, not a tic. When Nic is in real pain, the gaming metaphors disappear. They return when he breathes.

**Rule:** Max 2-3 gaming references per chapter. Zero in moments of real pain. They must signal emotional distance, not decorate the prose.

### 3.2 Constant self-deprecation

AI understands Nic is self-deprecating and produces continuous self-deprecation without variation.

**AI pattern:** Every paragraph has a joke about himself. The tone is uniformly light. Nic sounds like a comedian, not a person.

**How it works in Nic:** Self-deprecation is armor. When the armor drops, the tone changes completely. "Ce told me in late March. The Friday before you started..." — no irony here. Just weight. The contrast between the ironic moments and the naked ones is what makes Nic believable.

**Rule:** Every chapter must have at least 1 moment where the self-deprecation disappears completely. If Nic is always funny, he's not Nic — he's a mask.

### 3.3 Poetic sexual introspection

When writing explicit first-person male scenes, AI oscillates between two extremes: too clinical or too poetic. Nic is neither.

**AI poetic pattern:** "I lost myself in her body like a warm ocean, each wave of pleasure higher than the last."

**AI clinical pattern:** "I entered her with a decisive movement. She responded with a moan."

**How Nic writes:** "We come too fast. Too much hunger." Sex is told in the same voice as everything else — observation + self-deprecation + honesty. The body is a fact, not a metaphor. The emotion is in the context, not in the description of the act.

**Rule:** In explicit scenes, Nic's voice doesn't change register. Same rhythm, same irony, same honesty. If the narrator suddenly becomes "poetic" during sex, it's AI.

### 3.4 Too much emotional awareness

AI produces a Nic who always knows exactly what he feels and why. Real Nic is often confused, late to his own emotions, or in denial.

**AI pattern:** "I understood that the jealousy I felt stemmed from my fear of abandonment, rooted in my father's death."

**How Nic works:** "Jealousy? No. Or at least, not the kind I expected." Nic questions, denies, corrects, arrives at a half-answer. Full awareness only comes in therapy (CRI arc onward), and even there it's partial, contested, fragile.

**Rule:** Pre-CRI Nic has no therapy vocabulary. Post-CRI Nic has it but uses it with skepticism. In no arc does Nic "understand everything" in real time. Understanding always lags behind experience.

## PART 4 — Operational checklist

### For the writer agent (prevention)

Before generating a chapter:

- [ ] No words from §1.1 (significance inflation)
- [ ] No words from §1.3 (AI vocabulary)
- [ ] Max 1 participial emotional appendix per 2,000w (§1.2)
- [ ] "Is" used where it works (§1.4)
- [ ] Triads broken or reduced to two (§1.5)
- [ ] Max 3 em dashes per chapter (§1.6)
- [ ] Zero negative parallelisms (§1.7)
- [ ] Zero synonym cycling for character names (§1.8)

### For the reviewer agent (post-writing audit)

After every chapter:

**English prose (PART 1)**
- [ ] Flag words from §1.1: must be 0
- [ ] AI vocabulary from §1.3: must be 0
- [ ] Participial emotional appendices: ≤1 per 2,000w
- [ ] Copula avoidance ("serves as", "stands as"): must be 0
- [ ] Unbroken triads: must be 0
- [ ] Em dashes: ≤3
- [ ] Negative parallelisms: must be 0
- [ ] Synonym cycling for character references: must be 0

**First-person fiction (PART 2)**
- [ ] Emotions named AND explained in same sentence: must be 0 (§2.1)
- [ ] Linear thought without detours for >500 words: flag (§2.2)
- [ ] Symmetrical dialogue exchanges for >3 lines: flag (§2.3)
- [ ] Generic romance vocabulary in intimate scenes: must be 0 (§2.4)
- [ ] Profanity: 3-6 per 2,000w, irregular distribution (§2.5)
- [ ] Chapter closing with "realized/understood/knew/grasped": must be 0 (§2.6)
- [ ] Narrative transitions >10 words: flag (§2.7)
- [ ] Voice test: cover names, identify speakers (§2.8)

**Nic's voice (PART 3)**
- [ ] Gaming metaphors: ≤3, zero in moments of real pain (§3.1)
- [ ] Self-deprecation: present BUT with at least 1 naked moment (§3.2)
- [ ] Sexual register: identical to rest of chapter (§3.3)
- [ ] Emotional awareness: consistent with position in scar graph (§3.4)

### Scoring

| Level | Violations | Action |
|-------|:----------:|--------|
| ✅ Clean | 0-2 minor | Publishable |
| 🟡 Touch-up | 3-5 minor or 1 major | Rewrite flagged sections |
| 🔴 Rewrite | 6+ or 2+ major | Chapter reads as AI. Rewrite from scratch. |

**Major violations** (any occurrence = 🔴):
- Closing with "realized/understood/knew" (§2.6)
- Emotion named + explained + metaphor in same sentence (§2.1 + §1.2)
- Nic "understands everything" in real time (§3.4)
- All voices identical in a dialogue scene (§2.8)
- Register shift during sex (§3.3)

## PART 5 — The final test

After the mechanical audit (checklist), a qualitative test:

**"What makes this chapter obviously AI-generated?"**

Answer in 3-5 bullet points. If the answer is "nothing", the chapter passes. If there are points, rewrite and repeat.

Then:

**"Could this chapter appear in any English-language romance, or is it recognizably Eros?"**

If the answer is "any romance" → the chapter has lost its voice. Return to the arc's voice notes and rewrite.

| Word/phrase | Why |
|-------------|-----|
| pivotal, crucial, vital, fundamental | Significance inflation |
| represents, embodies, symbolizes | Significance inflation |
| underscores, highlights, showcases | Significance inflation |
| additionally, furthermore, moreover | AI connectors |
| delve, navigate (figurative), landscape (abstract) | AI vocabulary |
| tapestry, interplay, intricacies | AI vocabulary |
| foster, cultivate, garner | AI vocabulary |
| testament, beacon, enduring | AI vocabulary |
| it's important to note that | Filler |
| in this context | Filler |
| ultimately | Filler |
| a mix of X and Y | Emotion-labeling shortcut |
| serves as, stands as, functions as | Copula avoidance |
| not only X but also Y | Negative parallelism |
| on one hand... on the other | Negative parallelism |

| Dead metaphor | Why |
|---------------|-----|
| knot in his stomach | Generic |

## Appendix B — Adaptation for female POVs

When the POV is female (Ale, Cri, Ce, Vi, Angi, Ro), PART 2 and 3 rules adapt:

- §2.1 (articulated emotions): applies equally. Eros women aren't more articulate than Nic — they're articulate DIFFERENTLY.
- §2.4 (physical descriptions): vocabulary changes but rule stands. Ale describes Nic through specific details (his hands, his shirt, the way he drives), not generic adjectives.
- §2.5 (profanity): calibrate per character. Ale swears in Venetian dialect when she breaks. Cri is vulgar with elegance. Ce barely swears at all.
- §3.1-3.4: don't apply. Each female character has her own vocal tics defined in the arc structures.

**The universal rule remains:** if the reader can't tell who's speaking without dialogue tags, the voice is wrong.

## Appendix C — Adaptation notes for Italian translation

When translating EN chapters to Italian, apply the Italian-specific guide (`anti-ai-voice-guide.md`) as an additional layer. Key differences:

| EN signal | IT equivalent | Note |
|-----------|--------------|------|
| AI vocabulary (§1.3) | Different word list (§1.1, §1.3 in IT guide) | "Delve" doesn't translate, but "fondamentale" is equally AI in Italian |
| Participial phrases (§1.2) | Gerundio esplicativo (§1.2 in IT guide) | Italian gerunds are even more AI-flagged than English participles |
| Passive voice (not flagged in EN) | Flagged in IT guide §1.4 | Italian is more active than English — passives stand out more |
| Em dash limit: 3 | Em dash limit: 2 | Italian uses em dashes less than English |
| Profanity calibration | Same counts, different words | Italian profanity is more varied and register-specific |

The translation phase is a second opportunity to catch AI signals that survived the EN review.
