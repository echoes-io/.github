# Anti-AI Voice Check

## Purpose

The reader pays for a human voice. If it sounds generated, the pact breaks. This skill identifies and fixes the most common AI writing patterns.

## The Patterns to Kill

### 1. Meaning Inflation

AI loves to make everything "profound" and "pivotal."

**Kill these words/phrases:**
- pivotal, fundamental, profound, visceral
- "a weight settled in his chest"
- "something shifted between them"
- "in that moment, everything changed"
- "a realization washed over him"

**Fix:** Be specific. What EXACTLY shifted? Name the specific thought, not the category.

### 2. Gerund Cascades

AI overuses -ing constructions to pack simultaneous actions.

**Pattern:** "Walking through the door, feeling the weight of the day, knowing that..."

**Fix:** Sequential actions. One thing happens, then another. Humans don't narrate three simultaneous sensations.

### 3. Named Emotions

AI tells you what the character feels instead of showing it.

**Pattern:** "He felt sadness." "Anger rose in her chest." "A sense of longing filled him."

**Fix:** Show through body, action, thought. "His jaw tightened" not "he felt angry."

### 4. Artificial Symmetry

AI loves parallel structures, especially in threes.

**Pattern:** "She was beautiful, dangerous, and utterly captivating."

**Fix:** Break the pattern. Asymmetric descriptions feel human. One detail, developed, beats three adjectives listed.

### 5. Generic Openings

AI defaults to atmospheric scene-setting.

**Pattern:** "The morning light filtered through the curtains." "The city hummed with life below."

**Fix:** Start with action, thought, or dialogue. Drop the reader INTO the scene, not above it.

### 6. Reflective Closings

AI wraps up with a neat emotional bow.

**Pattern:** "And in that moment, he understood." "She smiled, knowing that everything would be different now."

**Fix:** End ON the moment. Cut the reflection. Let the reader feel it themselves.

### 7. Hedging Language

AI softens everything with qualifiers.

**Pattern:** "Something like a smile." "Almost imperceptibly." "A kind of sadness."

**Fix:** Commit. "A smile." "She shifted." "Sadness." (Or better: show it instead of naming it.)

### 8. Dialogue Attribution Overload

AI adds emotional stage directions to every line.

**Pattern:** '"I don't know," she said softly, her voice barely above a whisper, eyes downcast.'

**Fix:** Trust the dialogue. Action beats > adverbs. One physical detail per exchange, not three.

## The Test

Read your chapter aloud. At any point where you think "an AI would write it exactly like this" — rewrite that line.

## Quick Scan Checklist

- [ ] No word appears that you wouldn't use in conversation with a friend
- [ ] No sentence could be the output of "write me a literary paragraph about X"
- [ ] Opening line is specific, not atmospheric
- [ ] Closing line is action/image, not reflection
- [ ] Emotions are shown, never named
- [ ] No more than one simile per 500 words
- [ ] Humor exists somewhere (even dark humor)
