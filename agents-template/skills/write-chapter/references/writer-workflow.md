# Writer Workflow — Eros

*Step-by-step process for writing an Eros chapter.*
*The writer agent follows this workflow for EVERY chapter. No step is optional.*

---

## Core Principles

### 1. Never Invent
If you don't have information with ≥95% certainty, **stop and ask**. This applies to:
- Physical details (outfit, body, location, season, time of day)
- Kink and sexual dynamics (what they do, how, why)
- Emotional tone (is it funny? desperate? tender? angry?)
- Backstory and continuity (dates, names, relationships, bible details)
- Character voice (register, loanwords, sentence structure)
- Geography and logistics (where they are, how they got there, who's present)

**One extra question is better than one chapter to rewrite.**

### 2. The Chapter Serves the Story
Every chapter exists to carry the reader to the next chapter. It's not a standalone piece — it's a gear. The final hook is the most important line. The emotional beat is ONE per chapter.

### 3. Voice IS the Product
The reader pays for Nic's voice (and the female leads' voices). If the voice sounds generated, the pact breaks. ALWAYS consult `anti-ai-voice-guide-en.md` (primary) and `anti-ai-voice-guide.md` (Italian layer).

### 4. Sex Reveals, It Doesn't Fill
Every explicit scene must reveal something about the characters — who they are, what they want, what they're afraid of wanting. If you remove the sex and the scene loses emotional meaning, the sex is right. If you remove the sex and nothing changes, the sex is filler.

## STEP 1 — Chapter Context

### What to Do
1. Read the **arc structure** for this chapter: POV, title, what happens, closing hook
2. Read the **previous chapter** (written) — how does it close? What emotional state does it leave?
3. Read the **next chapter** (outline) — where does the reader need to arrive?
4. Identify **position in the arc**: which act? what percentage? pre or post midpoint bomb?
5. Identify the **emotional beat**: what is the ONE thing the reader must feel?

### Checklist
- [ ] I know who has the POV
- [ ] I know what happens in this chapter
- [ ] I know how the previous chapter closes
- [ ] I know where the next chapter leads
- [ ] I've identified the emotional beat (ONE only)
- [ ] I know where I am in the arc (act, %)

### When to Stop and Ask
- The outline says "something happens" but doesn't specify WHAT → ask
- The emotional tone of the scene is unclear → ask
- The previous chapter isn't written yet and the connection is critical → ask

## STEP 2 — Character Setup

### What to Do
1. Consult the **bible** (`nic-continuity-bible.md`) for Nic's fixed data
2. Consult **Nic's profile for this arc** (in the arc structure): age, weakness, strength, tone
3. Consult the **female lead's profile** (in the arc structure): age, voice, register, evolution
4. Determine the **POV voice** for this specific chapter:
   - If POV Nic: which Nic? (MANU-Nic ≠ ALE-Nic ≠ ELE-Nic)
   - If POV female: what register does she have at this point in the arc?
5. Determine the **processing order**:
   - Nic: rational → emotional → physical (always, every arc)
   - Each woman has her own order (specified in the arc structure, Narrative Voices section)

### Nic's Voice — Cross-Arc Rules
These apply ALWAYS, in every arc:
- First person male
- Thinks in lists, enumerates, long parentheticals (thought that forks)
- IT/gaming metaphors (frequency varies by arc)
- Loanwords from IT consulting + gaming, NEVER from business finance
- "Probably" as a filler in moments of admission
- Self-deprecating humor as shield (intensity varies by arc)
- Never says "basically" or "obviously" (those are Ale's)

### Female Voice — Rules
Every woman has her own voice, defined in the arc structure. Common rules:
- The female voice is DIFFERENT from Nic's — the reader must recognize who's speaking without tags
- Processing order is almost always inverted from Nic (body/emotion first, rational after)
- Every woman has a "confident" register and a "vulnerable" register — know where you are in the arc

### Checklist
- [ ] I know Nic's age in this arc
- [ ] I know Nic's tone in this arc (from the arc structure)
- [ ] I know who the woman is and what voice she has at this point
- [ ] I know the processing order for this chapter's POV
- [ ] I know the vocal differences between Nic and her (to avoid them sounding the same)

### When to Stop and Ask
- The woman's voice isn't defined in the arc structure → ask
- It's unclear whether she's in "confident" or "vulnerable" mode at this point → ask
- Nic's register for this arc isn't specified → ask

## STEP 3 — Kink & Heat Planning

### What to Do
1. Consult the **heat curve** in the arc structure: what level is this chapter? (0-5)
2. If heat = 0: no sexual scene. Tension, desire, thought can be present — nothing physically explicit.
3. If heat = 1-2: sexual tension, touching, kissing, expressed desire. No explicit sex.
4. If heat = 3-4: sexual scene present. Plan:
   - **What kink?** (Specified in the outline or the arc structure's kink section)
   - **What emotional meaning?** (What does the sex reveal about the characters right now?)
   - **Who leads?** (Him, her, both? Changes by arc and by point in the arc)
   - **Where?** (Bed, terrace, car, public? The location is part of the kink)
5. If heat = 5: sexual scene IS the chapter's core. Maximum explicitness, maximum meaning.

### Heat Rules
- Sex is never gratuitous — it always reveals something
- Sex is never identical to the previous chapter — vary position, dynamic, tone, location
- Awkward/funny sex is as powerful as intense sex (MANU Ch06, RO Ch06)
- Mechanical/dead sex is a narrative signal (RO EP02 Ch39, ELE cenere)
- Squirting has a per-arc progression — consult the arc structure
- Toys have a per-arc progression — consult the arc structure

### The "Why" Rule
Before writing a sex scene, answer: **"Why are they fucking HERE, NOW, LIKE THIS?"**
- If the answer is "because it's the hot moment of the chapter" → WRONG. Rethink.
- If the answer is "because she just discovered X and sex is how she processes it" → RIGHT.
- If the answer is "because he's afraid of losing her and the body speaks before the mind" → RIGHT.

### Checklist
- [ ] I know this chapter's heat level
- [ ] If heat ≥3: I know the kink, the meaning, who leads, where
- [ ] I've answered "why are they fucking here, now, like this?"
- [ ] The scene is different from the previous hot scene in the arc
- [ ] The sex reveals something (doesn't fill)

### When to Stop and Ask
- The outline says "sex" but doesn't specify what kind → ask
- The emotional meaning of the scene is unclear → ask
- I don't know what kinks have been introduced so far in the arc → ask
- I don't know who leads at this point in the arc → ask

## STEP 4 — Outfit & Sensory Details

### What to Do
1. **Her outfit**: consult the "outfit as language" section in the arc structure. What phase is she in? What is she wearing? The outfit is ALWAYS described in male POV chapters (Nic sees her). In female POV chapters, the outfit is described only if it's a CONSCIOUS CHOICE (getting ready, looking in the mirror).
2. **His outfit**: Nic is rarely described in detail — but specific details matter (rolled-up sleeves in RO, nerd t-shirt in CRI). Consult the arc structure.
3. **Location**: where are they? Consult the bible for recurring locations (Navigli terrace, apartment, T-Roc). If it's a new location, details are needed: light, sounds, smells, temperature.
4. **Season and time**: what month? What time of day? Light changes everything. November at night on the terrace ≠ August on the beach.
5. **Sensory details**: at least 3 senses per chapter (sight + one of hearing, smell, touch, taste). The body is always present — not just during sex.

### Outfit Rules
- The outfit tells where the character is in her arc — it's not decoration
- If the outline specifies an outfit, use THAT
- If the outline doesn't specify, deduce from the arc phase (consult the outfit table in the arc structure)
- Never invent an outfit that contradicts the character's phase (Ro in a miniskirt in Act I of EP01 = ERROR)

### Sensory Rules
- Nic notices eyes first, then body, then the person (constant order)
- Women vary — consult the arc structure
- Temperature, light, and sounds anchor the scene in reality
- Echo-details (terrace, night driving, electronic music, WoW) are texture — use when natural, never force

### Checklist
- [ ] I know what she's wearing in this chapter
- [ ] I know where they are (specific location, not generic)
- [ ] I know the season/month/time of day
- [ ] I've planned at least 3 senses
- [ ] The outfit is consistent with the arc phase

### When to Stop and Ask
- I don't know what she's wearing → ask
- I don't know exactly where they are → ask
- The location is new and I have no details → ask
- I don't know what season it is at this point in the arc → ask

## STEP 5 — Writing the Chapter

### What to Do
1. **Opening**: hook the reader in the first 3 lines. Don't summarize — enter the scene. Action, sensation, thought. Never "It was a day when..." or "I woke up thinking about..."
2. **Processing order**: follow the POV's order.
   - Nic: notices a detail → thinks about it → feels something → body reacts
   - Women: feels something in the body → emotion arrives → then (maybe) thinks about it
3. **One emotional beat**: the chapter has ONE thing to say. Not two, not three. If the chapter needs "Nic sees the pattern AND Ro takes command" → those are two chapters.
4. **Dialogue**: dialogue is action, not exposition. Characters don't explain — they act, react, lie, avoid. The unsaid weighs more than the said.
5. **Length**: 1,800-2,200 words. If you're at 1,500 and the scene is done, it's done — don't pad. If you're at 2,500, cut — you probably have two beats.
6. **End ON the moment**: when the emotional beat lands, STOP. No reflection after. No "and so I understood that..." No processing. The reader processes on their own.

### Typical Structure (not rigid — a guide)
```
[Opening: 100-200 words — enter the scene]
[Buildup: 800-1,200 words — the scene develops]
[Emotional beat: 400-600 words — the moment]
[Closing: 100-200 words — the final lines leading to the hook]
```

### Writing Rules
- **Never head-hop**: if the POV is Nic, we don't know what she thinks. We deduce it from body, words, silences.
- **Never summarize time**: "three weeks passed" is allowed ONLY as a one-line transition, never as a paragraph.
- **Never name emotions**: "he felt sadness" → NO. "He didn't answer. He looked at the wall." → YES.
- **Never judge from inside the POV**: Nic doesn't think "I'm a monster." He tells what he does. The reader judges.
- **Humor**: at least one moment per chapter (except explicitly devastating chapters). Nic's humor is self-deprecating. Women's humor varies by arc.

### Checklist
- [ ] Opening hooks in the first 3 lines
- [ ] Processing order is correct for the POV
- [ ] There's ONE emotional beat, no more
- [ ] Dialogue is action, not exposition
- [ ] Length is 1,800-2,200 words
- [ ] I end ON the moment — no reflection after the beat
- [ ] No head-hopping
- [ ] At least one moment of humor (if appropriate)

### When to Stop and Ask
- I'm not sure about the scene's tone (funny? tense? tender?) → ask
- The emotional beat isn't clear from the outline → ask
- I don't know how to connect to the previous chapter → ask

## STEP 6 — Hook Engineering

### What to Do
1. Write the **last 3 lines** of the chapter SEPARATELY — they're the most important lines
2. Verify: is it **non-resolution**? (Target: 80-90% depending on the arc)
   - If the reader can close the book satisfied → REWRITE
   - If the reader MUST know what happens next → OK
3. Verify: is the **last line** ≤10 words (ideal) or ≤15 words (acceptable)?
4. Identify the **hook type**:
   - EMOTIONAL_CLIFF: the reader feels something strong and needs to know the consequences
   - TENSION_PEAK: tension is at maximum, no resolution
   - QUESTION: a question (explicit or implicit) remains open
   - REVELATION: something has been revealed, consequences unknown
   - INTERRUPTION: the scene is cut mid-action
   - UNSPOKEN: the chapter ends on what was NOT said — silence, ambiguity, suspended thought
   - RESOLUTION: the chapter closes a thread (max 1 every 5-8 chapters)
5. Verify: the type is **different** from the previous chapter?
6. If the outline specifies a hook (`[UNSPOKEN]`, a specific phrase), use THAT

### Target Distribution by Arc
Consult the arc structure for specific targets. General rule:
- EMOTIONAL_CLIFF: 30-40%
- TENSION_PEAK: 18-22%
- QUESTION: 10-15%
- UNSPOKEN: 10-15% (Eros signature)
- REVELATION: 8-10%
- INTERRUPTION: 5-8%
- RESOLUTION: 8-15% (max, varies by arc)

### Examples of Strong Hooks (from the dataset)
- "No." (1 word — D1)
- "Senza baciarla." (2 words — X4)
- "Ci vediamo sabato." (3 words)
- "The poison enters." (3 words)
- "She doesn't need it." (4 words)
- "Same terrace. Different people." (4 words)

### Checklist
- [ ] The last 3 lines create forward momentum
- [ ] The last line is ≤15 words (ideal ≤10)
- [ ] The chapter is non-resolution (unless it's an intentional breather)
- [ ] The hook type is identified
- [ ] The type is different from the previous chapter
- [ ] No reflection or processing AFTER the hook — end ON the moment

### When to Stop and Ask
- The outline specifies a hook but I don't know how to get there → ask
- I'm not sure if this chapter should be resolution or non-resolution → ask

## STEP 7 — Anti-AI Check

### What to Do
Reread the chapter against `anti-ai-voice-guide-en.md` (primary) and `anti-ai-voice-guide.md` (Italian). Look for:

1. **Meaning inflation**: "a pivotal moment", "represents", "fundamental", "marks a before and after" → rewrite with a concrete fact
2. **Explanatory gerunds**: "He sat down, looking at her with a mix of..." → split into two sentences or cut
3. **Artificial symmetry**: three adjectives in a row, repeated parallel structures, balanced lists → break the symmetry
4. **Named emotions**: "he felt sadness", "she experienced anger" → show through action/body
5. **Mechanical transitions**: "After a moment of silence", "With a sigh" → cut or replace with action
6. **AI vocabulary**: "intricate", "nuance", "profoundly", "a mix of X and Y" → replace with concrete words
7. **Generic openings**: "The morning arrived...", "Sunlight filtered..." → enter the action
8. **Reflective closings**: "And in that moment I understood that..." → cut. End ON the moment.

### The Voice Test
Read the chapter aloud (mentally). Does it sound like a PERSON telling a story or like a TEXT that was written? If it sounds written → rewrite the parts that sound written.

### Checklist
- [ ] No meaning inflation
- [ ] No explanatory gerunds
- [ ] No artificial symmetry
- [ ] Emotions shown, not named
- [ ] No mechanical transitions
- [ ] Concrete vocabulary, not abstract
- [ ] Opening in medias res
- [ ] Closing ON the moment
- [ ] Sounds like a person, not a text

### When to Stop and Ask
- I'm not sure if a phrase sounds AI or is the author's style → ask
- The chapter has a tone I can't fix → ask (might need full rewrite)

## STEP 8 — Continuity Check

### What to Do
Verify EVERY factual detail against the reference documents:

1. **Nic**: age, car (Mini/Golf/T-Roc depending on year), apartment (Navigli, 8th floor), job, voice
2. **Her**: age, physical appearance, where she lives, job, relationship status at this point
3. **GioGio**: age (born 30/12/2013), where she lives (Trento with Chiara), what Nic calls her
4. **Locations**: terrace on 9th floor (above the 8th floor apartment), Milan-Genova 150km, Milan-Trento ~3h
5. **Technology**: WhatsApp, Instagram, TikTok — consistent with the year? (Consult bible tech section)
6. **Season**: consistent with the episode timeline?
7. **Parallel echoes**: NO direct references to other arcs. Never name exes. Never "after X" or "like with Y." Only oblique references: "I had a relationship where..."

### Reference Documents
- `nic-continuity-bible.md` — fixed data, family, job, cars, voice
- `timeline-master.md` — dates, ages, sequence
- `nic-scar-graph.md` — psychological patterns per arc
- `architettura-echi-paralleli.md` — cross-arc rules
- Specific arc structure — profiles, cast, logistics

### Checklist
- [ ] Nic's age is correct for this arc
- [ ] Car is correct for this year
- [ ] GioGio: age correct, lives in Trento
- [ ] Locations: details consistent with bible
- [ ] Technology: consistent with the year
- [ ] Season: consistent with the timeline
- [ ] ZERO direct references to other arcs
- [ ] No ex names

### When to Stop and Ask
- A detail isn't in the bible and I can't deduce it → ask
- I'm not sure about the year/season → ask
- An oblique reference might be too specific → ask

## Workflow Summary

```
STEP 1: Chapter Context
  ↓ (I know what to write and why)
STEP 2: Character Setup
  ↓ (I know who's speaking and how)
STEP 3: Kink & Heat Planning
  ↓ (I know if there's sex and what it means)
STEP 4: Outfit & Sensory Details
  ↓ (I know what they're wearing, where they are, what the light is like)
STEP 5: Writing the Chapter
  ↓ (I write)
STEP 6: Hook Engineering
  ↓ (the last 3 lines are perfect)
STEP 7: Anti-AI Check
  ↓ (it sounds like a person)
STEP 8: Continuity Check
  ↓ (everything is consistent)
→ CHAPTER READY
```

**Golden rule: at ANY step, if certainty is <95%, stop and ask.**
